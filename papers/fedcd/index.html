FedCD: A Fairness-aware Federated Cognitive Diagnosis Framework

Shangshang Yang, Jialin Han, Xiaoshan Yu, Ziwen Wang, Hao Jiang, Haiping Ma*, Xingyi Zhang, Geyong Min
ACM Transactions on Information Systems, 2026
TOIS 2026
PDF Paper arXiv Code Homepage

Abstract

With the rapid development of Internet technologies, online intelligent education platforms have generated a vast amount of distributed student learning data. This influx of data presents opportunities for cognitive diagnosis (CD) to assess students' mastery of knowledge concepts while also raising significant data privacy and security challenges. To cope with this issue, federated learning (FL) becomes a promising solution by jointly training models across multiple local clients without sharing their original data. However, the data quality problem, caused by the ability differences and educational context differences between different groups/schools of students, further poses a challenge to the client fairness of models. To address this challenge, this paper proposes a fairness-aware federated cognitive diagnosis framework (FedCD) to jointly train CD models built upon a novel parameter decoupling-based personalization strategy, preserving privacy of data and achieving precise and fair diagnosis of students on each client. As an FL paradigm, FedCD trains a local CD model for the students in each client based on its local student learning data, and each client uploads its partial model parameters to the central server for parameter aggregation according to the devised innovative personalization strategy. The main idea of this strategy is to decouple model parameters into two parts: the first is used as locally personalized parameters, containing diagnostic function-related model parameters, to diagnose each client's students fairly; the second is the globally shared parameters across clients and the server, containing exercise embedding parameters, which are updated via fairness-aware aggregation, to alleviate inter-school unfairness. Experiments on three real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed FedCD framework and the personalization strategy compared to five FL approaches under three CD models.

Contributions

  • Proposes FedCD, a fairness-aware federated cognitive diagnosis framework that jointly trains CD models across schools/clients without sharing raw student learning data.
  • Devises a parameter decoupling-based personalization strategy: student embeddings and the diagnostic function stay locally personalized, while exercise embeddings are globally shared across clients and the server.
  • Designs a fairness-aware aggregation mechanism that weights clients by their local prediction loss, alleviating inter-school unfairness caused by data-quality differences.
  • Validates the framework on three real-world datasets under three base CD models against five FL baselines, showing superior accuracy and fairness.

Method

Each communication round consists of four steps: ① each school/client trains its own CD model, initializing its local exercise embedding with the globally shared embeddings, and computes the predicted loss on local students; ② each client uploads only its exercise embedding to the central server; ③ the server determines aggregation weights in a fairness-aware manner (loss-based weighting) and aggregates the local exercise embeddings into a global exercise embedding; ④ the server distributes the global exercise embedding back to all clients, while student embeddings and the diagnostic function never leave the client.

Experimental Results

Compared algorithms: Five federated learning approaches — FedAvg, FedAtt, LoAdaBoost, F2MF, and PFedRec — under three base CD models (DINA, NCD, and KaNCD), plus GUM with HPFL.

Datasets: Three real-world datasets — ASSIST2009, ASSIST2012, and SLP-Math — under 60%/40%, 70%/30%, and 80%/20% train/test ratios.

Metrics: Diagnosis ACC, RMSE, and AUC, together with group fairness (GF) and degree of agreement (DOA) for fairness evaluation.

Overall results: FedCD achieves the best ACC/RMSE/AUC in most settings (e.g., it wins 27 out of 30 comparisons against FedAvg on ASSIST2009), and substantially reduces the performance disparity across schools with different data qualities, confirming both the diagnosis precision and the client fairness of the framework.
